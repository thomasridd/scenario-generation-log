Scenario Log Design & migration guide — v1

Off GOV.UK, onto our own look and feel

Scenario Log was prototyped on GOV.UK Frontend with the MoJ pattern library. That gave us accessibility and clarity for free — and a visual identity that isn't ours, plus a Crown-branded footer on an internal tool. This guide keeps the discipline and replaces the identity. Palette and layout ideas are drawn from methods.co.uk: white canvas, purple blocks, grey panels.

Principles

01

Keep the rigour

One column of content, generous type, visible focus, real HTML semantics, error summaries at the top. Nothing here relaxes WCAG 2.2 AA.

02

Drop the crown

No black GOV.UK masthead, no Crown copyright, no OGL licence, no GDS Transport, no #1d70b8 blue. Using them off gov.uk is misleading and, for the font, not licensed.

03

Purple carries the brand

One brand colour doing structural work — header rule, primary action, headings on blocks. Grey does the panels. White does everything else.

04

It's a tool, not a service

Scenario Log is dense, repeat-visit, internal. Tables get tabular figures and monospace identifiers; filters stay put; density is allowed to rise.

Type

GDS Transport must go. Public Sans replaces it — open licence, designed for dense public-sector interfaces, close enough in temperament that nothing breaks, different enough that nobody mistakes this for GOV.UK. IBM Plex Mono carries identifiers, IDs and timestamps so they can be scanned and compared.

Rules

  • Headings 700, tight tracking. No 300 weights anywhere.
  • Numbers in tables: font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums.
  • Measure caps at 70ch for prose.
  • Sentence case for headings and buttons. No title case.
  • Section eyebrows are 15px, 700, uppercase, 0.14em tracked — the one uppercase device.

Space, shape, motion

4px scale

4 · 8 · 12 · 16 · 24 · 32 · 48 · 64. Page gutter 32px mobile, 56px desktop. Max content width 1100px — wider than GOV.UK's 960px because tables need it.

Square corners, flat surfaces

Radius 0 everywhere. No drop shadows, no gradients, no cards floating on tinted backgrounds. Separation comes from 2px rules, 2px gaps and grey fills — the block language from the Methods site.

Motion

120ms colour and border transitions on interactive elements. Nothing moves position, nothing fades in on load, everything respects prefers-reduced-motion. Focus is instant — never animated.

Migration

Five phases, each shippable on its own. The full instructions — token file, class-by-class overrides, files to touch, acceptance checks — are in MIGRATION.md, written for Claude Code to work through in order.

Phase 1

Tokens

Add tokens.css with custom properties and the two webfonts. Nothing visibly changes.

Phase 2

Chrome

Replace header and footer templates. Crown, OGL and the black bar come out. Highest-visibility, lowest-risk win.

Phase 3

Type & colour

Override the GOV.UK link, focus, body and heading rules. GDS Transport removed from the build.

Phase 4

Components

Buttons, tags, tables, panels, notifications, summary lists. MoJ filter panel restyled or replaced.

Phase 5

Cut the dependency

Drop unused govuk/MoJ CSS, keep the JS behaviours worth keeping, run the accessibility checklist.

Non-negotiables for whoever does the work

  • No GOV.UK or Crown branding, no OGL licence statement, no GDS Transport, no #1d70b8 — anywhere, including favicons, meta theme-color and print styles.
  • Focus states are never removed or restyled to something weaker than yellow + ink underline.
  • Every status conveyed by colour also carries a word.
  • Contrast is checked, not assumed: 4.5:1 body text, 3:1 non-text boundaries and 44px minimum targets.
  • Keep GOV.UK's markup and JS behaviours (error summary focus, skip link, character count, accessible autocomplete) even after the styles diverge.
